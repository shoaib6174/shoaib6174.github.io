---
title: "MicroMasters in Supply Chain Management, five graduate-level courses"
---

MITx, 2020 to 2021. Five graduate-level courses from MIT's supply chain program, each about thirteen weeks, taken while finishing the textile degree. This is where the probability, statistics, and optimisation I later needed for machine learning came from, and where I first saw machine learning applied to a business problem.

**Supply Chain Analytics.** Probability and statistics for modelling variability, hypothesis testing and regression, mathematical optimisation for planning and network design, simulation for evaluating policies under uncertainty, approximation methods for problems too large to solve exactly.

**Supply Chain Fundamentals.** Demand forecasting, inventory replenishment policies, transportation routing and modal choice, segmenting customers, products, and channels, managing uncertainty and risk.

**Supply Chain Design.** Network design: transportation, transshipment, facility location. Supply chain finance: activity-based costing, working capital, the cash-to-cash cycle, discounted cash flow, and translating operational decisions into financial terms. Supplier management, procurement, contracts. Production planning, bills of materials, material requirements planning. Sales and operations planning.

**Supply Chain Dynamics.** System dynamics modelling, the MIT method for non-linear dynamic systems. Sources of complexity and interdependence, risk and resilience, international operations, the trade-offs between efficiency, flexibility, and resilience.

**Supply Chain Technology and Systems.** Data modelling and database design, SQL on supply chain data, machine learning models for supply chain decisions, evaluating and implementing enterprise systems such as ERP, WMS, and TMS, emerging technology.
