Early in 2020 I stopped treating programming as a side project. I finished a web development bootcamp, run by an IPE graduate who had made the same switch, and learned from it that I did not enjoy frontend work. Google's IT Support certificate filled gaps in the basics. The cloud specialisation I took because DevOps was in demand, and finishing it told me DevOps was not for me either. Two doors closed, which was useful.

The door that opened was the MicroMasters in Supply Chain Management from MITx: five graduate-level courses over more than a year. Forecasting, inventory, transportation, network design, the finance of a supply chain, system dynamics, and underneath all of it the probability and statistics I had never had. One lecture in the fifth course was about machine learning in supply chains. That was the first time I had the prerequisites for something I wanted to learn.
