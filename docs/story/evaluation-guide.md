# Evaluation guide for the site's story and copy

Used by every review pass. Score each criterion 1–5 and quote the offending text for anything below 4. A page passes when nothing scores below 4 and nothing in the hard-fail list is present.

## Hard fails (any one fails the page)

- Private items present: IUT admission or finances, family circumstances around the PhD, undergraduate CGPA, faith, only child.
- A claim not supported by `docs/life-inventory.md`.
- Pitch language: passion, journey, spark, chapter, story, relentless, driven, dynamic, results-driven, leverage, empower, seamless, cutting-edge, next-generation, "I am a X who Y".
- Adjectives about his own character (persistent, curious, broad, entrepreneurial, analytical, passionate, dedicated) stated rather than shown. See the traits table in `portrayal.md` for how each is shown instead.
- A number that is not in the inventory, or a number without a source.
- A broken or unverified link (see the Link check section of the inventory).

## Criteria

### 1. Structure
- Does the page answer who, what, and why in that order, within the first screen?
- Is every section earning its place? Could a reader skip it without losing the thread?
- Are the three roles (builds, explains, organises) visible on the home page without being labelled as a framework?

### 2. Language
- First person. Past tense for things done, present for things happening.
- Short sentences, about 20 words or fewer. One idea per sentence.
- Headings are nouns and dates, not punchlines or questions.
- No semicolons chaining clauses, no em-dashes, no parentheses.
- Reads like a careful engineer describing himself, not a brochure. Test: would he say this aloud to a colleague?

### 3. Flow
- Each section leads to the next. Chronology is clear wherever time matters.
- Decisions are stated as decisions: what was chosen and what it taught, not what happened to him.
- The eliminations (frontend, DevOps, theory) read as useful outcomes, not failures.

### 4. Wording
- Specific nouns over general ones: "Nav2 waypoints" not "navigation tasks".
- Numbers only where they change what the reader thinks: 2,636 downloads, 736 tests, three volumes, fifty universities. No padding numbers.
- Names of people, institutions, and tools spelled as they spell themselves.
- Bengali titles given in transliteration with an English gloss in parentheses once.

### 5. Completeness
- Every facet from the inventory is represented somewhere: robots, building, quantum, writing, university, off the clock, skills.
- Skills appear with proof. Every skill group links to at least one item that demonstrates it.
- The four kept-in items are present: failed coding attempts, GSoC 2023 unfunded proposal, textile courses not enjoyed, things tried and ruled out.
- The books, the two papers, the Kaggle downloads, and the Medium stats are present where relevant.

### 6. Honesty and proportion
- Nothing overstated. "Paper in preparation for CoRL 2026" not "published at CoRL".
- Concept-stage work labelled as such (security robot: waiting on funding; CubeSat: engineering model next).
- Client work described as client work.

### 7. Reader test
- A recruiter: in 60 seconds, do they know what he does now, what he can do, and one reason to remember him?
- A lab PI: do they see research work with evidence (repo, tests, paper target)?
- A reader of his books or articles: do they find the writing within two clicks?

## Review procedure

1. Read the page copy top to bottom once without stopping.
2. Score the seven criteria. Quote text for every score below 4.
3. List hard fails.
4. Propose the smallest edit that fixes each quoted problem. Do not rewrite passages that pass.
5. The author applies edits, then the next reviewer starts from step 1 on the revised copy.

Three passes minimum per page. Stop when a pass produces no edits.
