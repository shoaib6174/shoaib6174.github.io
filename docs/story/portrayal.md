# Portrayal: how the site presents Mohammad Shoaib

Written before the story, used by every story review. The rule: honest in every fact, diplomatic in emphasis and order. Nothing here hides a fact; it decides which facts lead, which follow, and how each is worded so it is read the way it deserves.

## Emphasis, decided by Shoaib

Lead with **agentic AI and vision-language models on robots**: LLM-driven task planning and tool-calling on real robot stacks, VLM and VLA perception, edge deployment. Reinforcement learning work (pursuit-evasion, CoRL target) is present and credited but second. Business-side LLM systems (BotForge, guard pipelines, document agents) are the same skill applied off-robot.

## The critique: how a skeptical reader could take the raw log

Read cold, the inventory invites eight objections. Each is listed with the evidence a reader would point to.

1. **Scattered.** Textiles, web dev, supply chain, data analytics, NLP, video transformers, quantum, Kubernetes, fashion AI, HPC, robotics, chatbots. Nine certificates. Looks like someone who follows whatever is trending: quantum in 2023, LLM agents in 2025.
2. **Short tenures.** DeepPavlov four months, GSoC four months, NITEX seven months, TA one semester. A pattern of leaving.
3. **Nothing finished at depth.** One first-author research output is still "in preparation". The published papers are a section of a textile review and a student product-design paper. The MSc has run since 2023.
4. **Walked away from theory.** "Preferred building to theory" can be read as could not do the math, especially by a PI.
5. **Excuses.** "The organisation accepted my proposal but Google cut the funding" can sound like sour grapes. Four failed attempts at coding can sound like a confession rather than a credential.
6. **Inflated company.** "Co-founder of FenloAI" for contract work with two friends, with the flagship product's URL serving a Kubernetes certificate error, looks like a startup costume.
7. **Small numbers.** Fifteen thousand article views in five years and a few thousand downloads are modest. Stated as achievements they shrink the author.
8. **Student-era padding.** Debate at 20, a business club, a youth network: fine on a CV at 22, odd as headline material at 28.

## The answer: what is actually true, and the framing that lets it be read that way

### 1. Not scattered: convergent
Every step after 2020 reduced the search space. Web dev ruled out frontend. The cloud track ruled out DevOps. The MicroMasters supplied the math. NLP, then vision, then quantum, then robotics each narrowed what he wanted: AI systems that act in the physical world, built end to end. The site shows the convergence, not the breadth. **Rule:** the home page names what he does now in the first sentence and gives the route in six lines or fewer. Breadth lives on the Journey page as a sequence of decisions, each with what it taught.

### 2. Tenures: show what was delivered and why he left
Every departure in the log was a pull, not a push. NITEX ended because faculty asked him back. The TA post was a one-semester contract by design. GSoC and DeepPavlov are fixed-term by definition. **Rule:** roles are described by what shipped (two platforms, a model on TF Hub, a dialogue-graph pipeline) and dated by year, not month. The reason for each move is stated in one clause where it is a pull.

### 3. Depth: show the rigour, not the publication count
The pursuit-evasion codebase has 736 tests, 40 training runs, ablations, and a decision log. The task planner has session worklogs and a results tracker. That is research practice, visible in the repos. **Rule:** describe research by its evidence (repo, tests, runs, target venue) and label status plainly: "in preparation", "waiting for funding", "engineering model next". Never imply a publication that does not exist. The MSc is listed once, on Journey, without emphasis on duration.

### 4. Theory: a preference discovered and acted on
He went to NSU specifically to do quantum research, did a semester of it, and chose to build instead. That is self-knowledge, and for an applied robotics lab it is a qualification. **Rule:** state it as a decision in one sentence on Journey: "I enjoyed learning quantum computing and found I wanted to build systems rather than do theoretical research." No elaboration, no apology.

### 5. Setbacks: one sentence each, same format as successes
The GSoC 2023 line is a fact, not a grievance: "Proposal to QMLHEP. Selected by the organisation, outside that year's funded slots." The failed coding attempts are one line in the 2017 entry: "JavaScript, Java, C++, competitive programming, then CS50. None of it held." **Rule:** setbacks get the same dated-entry format as everything else, never a paragraph, never an explanation of whose fault it was. The reader draws the conclusion.

### 6. FenloAI: call it what it is
"Co-founder of FenloAI, where three of us build automation and AI agents for small businesses on contract." That is accurate and, said plainly, reads as someone who keeps shipping for real clients while doing research. **Rule:** no "startup", no "founder" without "co-". Products named only where they are live and verified. BotForge is not linked until its DNS is fixed.

### 7. Numbers: only the ones that stand alone
2,636 downloads of a model he ported is concrete and independent. Three volumes, category best seller, is a publisher fact. Fifty universities is a scope fact. Article views are shown per article on the Writing page for the top pieces only; no site-wide totals. **Rule:** a number appears only if a reader would understand it without comparison.

### 8. Student roles: proof of a habit, placed where they belong
Debate, the business club, TEXBIZ, and YSI are the evidence that he has organised people since 2017. They belong on the University page and in the Journey, with one line on Home. **Rule:** the home page says the habit once with the current evidence (FenloAI co-founder, Qiskit Advocate) and links to the history.

## The three roles, weighted

The persona stays: builds systems, explains what he learns, brings people together. But not equal thirds.

- **Builds** is the headline and most of the site. Specifically: agentic AI on robots, then LLM systems for businesses, then the earlier ML and infrastructure work.
- **Explains** is the second line: four books, the article series that follow each thing learned, the TA semester.
- **Brings people together** is the third line, stated once with current evidence, with the history on University.

Weighting avoids the trap plan C named: three verbs in the nav become a tagline.
